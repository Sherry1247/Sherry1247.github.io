Behind the work

About Me

I’m a Data Science and Computer Science student at UW–Madison with a Mathematics minor. I like the point where machine learning meets a person who needs it to work: diagnosing failing engine sensors, turning sign language into speech, and helping low-vision users read stairs and obstacles.

This fall I joined Prof. Diego Hernando’s imaging group (QIML) as an undergraduate research assistant, working with Yura on detecting and classifying liver disease from MRI localizer images. I also work as a student analyst in the campus Cybersecurity Operations Center, and I did accessibility research at MadAbility Lab.

If you want the short version, here is my résumé. If you want the long one, start with the projects, the experience timeline, or the course guide.

The thread

Notice. Translate. Guide.

Three projects, one habit: find the signal people miss, make sense of it, and hand it to someone who can use it.

01

Notice

A sensor that diagnoses—and repairs—itself.

Engine sensors fail quietly and corrupt everything downstream. I built a residual-based framework that identifies ten kinds of sensor faults, estimates severity, and reconstructs the damaged signal instead of just raising an alarm.

98.74% fault classification accuracy · ASME winner

Read the case
02

Translate

Sign language into speech, frame by frame.

As team lead, I guided four engineers through a browser-based ASL-to-speech pipeline: landmark tracking, Transformer recognition, language refinement, and spoken output.

2,044 sign classes · 11.59 ms average latency

Read the case
03

Guide

Making physical space legible without sight.

At MadAbility Lab, I explored how depth sensing, segmentation, and spatial feedback can help low-vision users understand stairs and obstacles.

D455 depth capture · stair detection prototype
